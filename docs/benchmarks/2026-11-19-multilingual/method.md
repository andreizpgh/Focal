# Multilingual review measurement

## Speech

The primary corpus contains 239 recordings. The project processes them locally using the pinned Parakeet Ultra INT8 model, sherpa-onnx 2.0.1, CPU greedy decoding with two threads, the native Silero VAD and Focal's Kotlin segmenter, window filter and formatter. The same saved tokens were passed through the formatter before and after the regional punctuation changes. The app detects the language itself. Language metadata below is used for evaluation.

- **182 short recordings.** Each of the 25 published Parakeet languages has separate development and test recordings. We used the raw, punctuated reference, required at least four seconds per clip and excluded filenames from Focal's earlier 84-clip experiment. Within a language there is no overlap of audio hashes, source sentence IDs or transcript hashes across the new splits. Google FLEURS (Conneau et al., CC BY 4.0) provides recording-level gender information. These samples establish coverage of real voices across languages. Per-language percentages have small denominators. [Google FLEURS, revision 70bb2e8](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/fleurs/tree/70bb2e84b976b7e960aa89f1c648e09c59f894dd)
- **50 continuous recordings.** For 24 languages we took two different videos from the uploader-captioned subset of ESPnet YODAS2 (CC BY 3.0). Selection required a continuous excerpt of 180–216 seconds, at least 190 written words and at least four candidate terminal marks. The second video is the holdout. Speaker identity is unavailable. The subtitle track may be translated, abbreviated or badly timed. We publish agreement for every recording. The primary punctuation comparison requires at least 70% exact word matches divided by the larger lexical word count. This selects easier-to-align audio and can also exclude bad recognition. Use the separately reported FLEURS WER for a comparison against controlled, same-language references. [ESPnet YODAS2, revision 631fc44](https://huggingface.co/datasets/espnet/yodas2/tree/631fc446cb1a41ee93a1d390a167c6e08c2597a3)
- **Maltese long speech.** The 50 include two consecutive publisher readings in Maltese with corresponding written paragraphs. Matthew 4 and 6 supply different passages of approximately 215 and 290 seconds. Published verse timing places the start after the spoken chapter heading. Two more Maltese parliamentary speeches are separate, shorter controls by the same speaker. [Maltese readings](https://www.jw.org/mt/librerija/bibbja/nwt/kotba/mattew/4/) · [European Parliament floor speech](https://huggingface.co/datasets/beshkenadze/europarl-floor-asr/tree/7ece831885d160d2de6273ce7a1e28098a789636)
- **Five language-switch controls.** Four real held-out FLEURS utterances alternate between two languages with 600 ms of silence between clips. They probe switching between utterances. Within-sentence borrowing, conversational timing and accents need independent recordings. Their component audio also appears in the short test and the controls have their own totals.
- **Supplemental readings.** When a YODAS2 pair failed the agreement threshold, we sought the corresponding 3–5 minute publisher reading in the same language. Chapter 4 is reserved for development-source failures and chapter 6 for holdout-source failures. Published verse marks determine the excerpt. The readings provide a punctuated text and authored paragraphs. Croatian and Latvian use 254–265 seconds of a publisher’s recorded magazine article with paragraph-level time marks. Different article IDs supply the Latvian development-source and holdout-source controls. They do not replace any primary result or alter the classifier's training data. Source, verse range and hashes are in the case receipts.

## Decisions and scoring

1. **Focal** retains its existing ASR punctuation and formatting. All note variants start from the same formatted words.
2. **Timing classifier.** A logistic regression uses seven coefficients including the intercept. Its six input features describe the word gap, two pause indicators, capitalization of the next word, low confidence on either side and words since the last sentence ending. Each language contributes equal total weight during development. One comparison uses a common 0.90 probability threshold. A second chooses a cutoff from 0.15 to 0.95 in 0.05 steps on development records, maximizing exact fixes minus introduced errors with a nonnegative result in each represented language. It abstains if there is no positive setting. It can suggest a period in an empty gap.
3. **Horizon Labs punctuation-restoration-small INT8.** The published multilingual ModernBERT graph takes cased text and predicts period, comma, question, dash, colon or no mark. All Focal languages appear in the model card. Each 96-subtoken block owns its words and uses up to 20 subtokens of context on either side. The last subtoken of each word supplies its label. The tokenizer's default truncation is disabled before windowing. At probability 0.90 or above the candidate can fill a whitespace gap. Existing marks, capitalization, words and paragraph layout stay in place. The Greek question sign is rendered as a semicolon. Exclamation marks and Spanish opening marks are outside this model's label set. [Model, revision a1818fa](https://huggingface.co/Horizon-Labs/punctuation-restoration-small/tree/a1818fae73a1d09b5496e67747dda7bd667b79a8)

**Word accuracy** counts unit-cost edits after NFC, case folding and letter/digit tokenization. **Punctuation** is scored between two consecutively aligned exact lexical matches. It counts the absence of a sign as a decision. The final sign and marks next to misrecognized words are unscored. Quotes and inverted openers are separated from trailing marks. Greek question signs compare as questions. There are separate counts for exact mark errors, missed/extra sentence endings, wrong inverted openers and paragraph breaks inside reference sentences. Authored paragraph metrics count matches, extra breaks and misses only where two exact matched words let us judge the break. Subtitle line changes supply no paragraph ground truth.

**Word review** counts whether each marked UI word agrees with the reference on a minimum-edit alignment. Recall uses displayed words with a lexical mismatch. Omitted reference words have no tap target. A false flag here can still represent an acceptable spoken variant. The spelling-menu probe uses the compiled Android reading screen's own flagged-span grouping and nearby-word selection. Each offered spelling is applied in isolation and rescored against the whole reference. Scores describe the effect of choosing an option. A second probe reconstructs the alternate reading from untrimmed context in visible decoded windows. It uses the same Kotlin window and word logic, then replays the menu’s timing, confidence and deduplication checks on the 101 primary holdout notes. Its per-option score uses the same single-change WER calculation.

## Repeat locally

Use the source IDs, excerpt boundaries and audio/reference hashes in [cases.jsonl](cases.jsonl). For each recording, retain its local WAV, reference, Focal's copy text and the aligned words. The evaluator reads a JSON list with `id`, `source`, `language`, `split` (`dev`, `holdout` or `control`), `draft`, `reference`, `audio` and `words`. Paths are local. Each UTF-8 words TSV row contains the displayed token, start and end milliseconds, confidence or `null`, and `true` or `false` for the app's doubtful flag. The case receipts contain source identifiers and hashes. The recordings, references, word timestamps and model weights are kept outside Git. Clip hashes refer to the local WAV used for processing. Re-encoding a WAV can change its container hash.

Install `tokenizers==0.22.2` and fetch the INT8 graph, tokenizer and config at the linked model revision. Build the CPU [native runner](../../../scripts/punctuation-bench/runner.c) against ONNX Runtime 1.28.2 following the [Russian replay instructions](../2026-11-19-punctuation/README.md#repeat-the-measurement).

```sh
python scripts/punctuation-bench/multilingual.py \
  --manifest "$LOCAL_MANIFEST" --model "$MODEL_DIR" --runner "$RUNNER" \
  --work "$LAB/replay" --output "$LAB/scores.json" --review-packet "$LAB/study"
```

For new control recordings use `--timing-weights "$LAB/scores.json"` to reuse the frozen fit. Replace `--runner` with `--logits "$LAB/replay/logits.bin"` to repeat scoring with identical token windows. To score the app's spelling menu, build `:app:assembleDebug`, compile [ReviewChoices.java](../../../scripts/punctuation-bench/ReviewChoices.java) against the app's compiled Kotlin classes, the core JAR and the Kotlin standard library, then pass it each private note directory containing `asr.txt` and `words.tsv`. Run [score_choices.py](../../../scripts/punctuation-bench/score_choices.py) with the same manifest and an output path.

For the overlap probe, [ReviewContext.java](../../../scripts/punctuation-bench/ReviewContext.java) uses the core JAR and Kotlin standard library on JDK 17. In each local note directory it reads raw mono 16 kHz PCM16LE (`pcm`), one Silero boolean byte per frame (`vad`), and the decoder's `tokens` TSV. Each token row contains the window WAV name (numeric stem), start seconds, duration seconds, log probability and hex-encoded UTF-8 spelling. Pass the same note directories as for the spelling menu. Run [overlap_choices.py](../../../scripts/punctuation-bench/overlap_choices.py) on the primary holdout manifest. The script needs the corresponding `choices.tsv` and `context.tsv` and does not require Android or a new decoding pass.

The tiny classifier's Android microbenchmark builds [TimingReplay.java](../../../scripts/punctuation-bench/TimingReplay.java) with Java 8 compatibility and D8 (API 26). Its private input contains a row with the seven frozen weights followed by one row per already prepared gap. Pass the file and the frozen cutoff to `app_process /system/bin TimingReplay` with the read-only DEX archive on its classpath.

## Reader timing

The optional private packet creates two worksheets per language and a separate assignment key. Give each fluent reader one worksheet, hide the key and use the same timer and audio controls for both conditions. Readers correct the text until they would copy it, record elapsed seconds, and count word, sign and paragraph changes. Readers see each passage once. The second reader receives the opposite version of each passage. Judge remaining errors against the references after both have finished. Report time per 100 words together with final accuracy and the number of readers. A separate usability comparison on Focal should counterbalance the inline review and full editor on unfamiliar, comparable passages. No participant times have been collected.
