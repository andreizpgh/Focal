# Transcript review and speech research

Focal keeps the original audio, the recognizer's tokens and the user's editable text separately. Parakeet Ultra INT8 produces the transcript offline. Every additional speech or punctuation model evaluated below ran in research tools outside the APK; raw JSON dumps and prototype harnesses from those experiments are archived in Git at tag `v0.4.0` (`cd859d6`).

## Current design in the app (0.5.0)

- **Unified page transitions.** Every note — single-line, wrapped, or truncated, from the Stage card or any library row — opens with a dual-layout crossfade flight using measured typography and overlapping alpha curves so mid-flight frames stay solid. Pulling the tonal-paper Stage card down interpolates the surface and title monotonically at every drag position.
- **Instant-keyboard in-place review.** Tapping any word, sweeping a phrase, or tapping **Review · N** in the top bar opens inline editing immediately with the keyboard ready and the target word or phrase selected. A compact single-row strip rides above the keyboard with **Listen**, one-tap suggestions (nearby spellings or any earlier short manual edit in the same note), and **Keep** / **Next** / **Apply**. Confirming a word with **Keep** (or **Mark all as reviewed** in the overflow menu) persists a per-word `reviewed` flag, clears its dotted underline, decrements **Review · N**, and offers Undo without modifying the text or raw tokens.
- **Playback speed.** The note player strip includes a **1× / 1.5× / 2×** speed toggle while keeping the word lens synced to the playback head.
- **Clean formatting.** Paragraphs break after 40 words at sentence boundaries or after 12 words at a 3-second pause in unpunctuated speech, without synthetic comma or period insertion. Window-overlap alternative readings were removed after the multilingual evaluation showed they fired rarely (4 of 227 spans) and hurt more than they helped. ## What the experiments established

Word-error counts compare a proposed replacement with a reference. A helpful option reduces word edit distance even if it only partly repairs a phrase. A tied option can still be wrong. A count of helpful options measures an opportunity to help, not the time a person saved.

| Experiment | Result and product decision |
| --- | --- |
| Local Qwen models, on original and stripped RU/EN drafts | On 12 public clips and 27 selected spans, neither Qwen3 0.6B nor Qwen3.5 2B improved wording. The 2B model made two harmful changes. It restored more punctuation on deliberately stripped input but did not improve the original ASR punctuation. The earlier audio-grounded Gemma 4 E2B test saved one word error on normal speech, saved none on quiet speech and invented words on noise. Neither result supports an automatic rewrite. [Qwen study](benchmarks/2026-11-19-review/README.md) · [Earlier correction evidence](speech-quality.md) |
| Compact Russian punctuation model | The INT8 package and vocabulary take 29.5 MiB. On two long monologues its conservative fill reduced punctuation discrepancies from 158 to 150. On the complete 17:48 classroom dictation they stayed at 20, with a 120 ms warm median on the two-core Android emulator. Replacing all marks raised discrepancies to 37 on that dictation. [Russian experiment and resource measurements](benchmarks/2026-11-19-punctuation/README.md) |
| All 25 languages, including timing-based and multilingual punctuation | On 84 short holdout recordings, Focal made 284 word edits in 1,824 reference words. The multilingual punctuation model and the shipped formatter both had 301 mark discrepancies at 7,044 scoreable short and long holdout boundaries. Timing alone added no net benefit there. Of 286 underlined words, 256 contained a visible mismatch, but the marks covered only 18.6% of the visible mismatches. Long primary holdout recordings with usable references covered 17 languages. [Per-language evidence and reader protocol](benchmarks/2026-11-19-multilingual/README.md) |
| Re-recognizing more audio around a tap | At 343 held-out selections the stricter rule added 34 spellings beyond the existing menu. Fifteen helped, 14 tied and five harmed. At 275 of 311 selections with enough surrounding audio it would run inference and return nothing. The median emulator wait was about seven seconds per check. [Wider-audio study](benchmarks/2026-11-20-context-review/README.md) |
| Alternatives at a single token in the first pass | The stricter rule added 19 new options. Four helped, 12 tied and three harmed. Even choosing perfectly among the entire eligible pool could help at only 16 of 343 selections. [Single-piece comparison](benchmarks/2026-11-21-first-pass/README.md) |
| Following alternatives to the end of the word | Complete continuations offered a helpful option at 52 of 343 selections. The strict first choice added 23 helpful, 97 tied and 25 harmful options. A small learned ranker offered three helpful, nine tied and three harmful options. All 415 baseline windows retained identical tokens, timing and confidence. Direct branch work cost a median 73 ms per window on the emulator. Standalone recognizer RSS peaked near 930 MiB in both conditions. These alternatives remain experimental. [Word continuations and decision model](benchmarks/2026-11-21-word-continuations/README.md) |

The datasets, splits, frozen thresholds, data limitations and reproduction steps are linked above. The research scripts operate on public fixtures or local intermediates. Android has no Internet permission and loads no research model. CPU timing from x86-64 emulation does not establish ARM latency or energy use.

## Priorities for the next evaluation

1. **Measure whether review saves time.** Counterbalance the reading screen and full editor on comparable unfamiliar passages. Measure seconds per 100 words, missed lexical errors, unnecessary taps and harmful accepted suggestions. Include spontaneous dictation, names, numbers, negation and language switches. Start with Russian and English and include languages with weaker results, especially Greek, Latvian and Maltese. Preserve independent speakers and report individual language results. [Reader protocol](benchmarks/2026-11-19-multilingual/method.md#reader-timing)
2. **Improve which words get underlined.** Compare confidence calibration and phrase grouping on fresh recordings. Evaluate visible precision and recall against the present 89.5% and 18.6% on the documented holdout. Count deletions separately since there is no displayed word to underline. Record misses that change meaning and distinguish a speaker mismatch from an unreliable reference.
3. **Improve suggestion ranking before expanding the menu.** Label competing spellings, including plausible but wrong alternatives, on independent audio. Compare against earlier manual edits and existing overlap choices. Evaluate new helpful choices beyond that menu, harmful first suggestions and performance on unseen languages. Revisit the small ranker only if improvements generalize.
4. **Validate paragraphing and punctuation with readers.** Add independent long recordings for the languages with missing or unreliable long references. Use naturally dictated and repunctuated transcripts. Measure both reading effort and boundaries placed inside a sentence. Keep punctuation changes independently reversible and preserve the exact lexical sequence.
5. **Measure the midrange phone budget.** Profile the signed build on ARM with 6 GB RAM or less. Measure cold model installation, CPU and process memory while capturing and reviewing, frame pacing on wrapped titles, time to final text, heat and battery over an hour with the screen off. Exercise call interruptions, background recovery and large notes. [Device checklist](testing.md#phone-validation)
